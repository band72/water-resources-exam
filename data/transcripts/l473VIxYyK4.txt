In this problem, a 600 megawatt coal fired power plant operates at full capacity with a thermal efficiency of 32%. The plant uses Illinois basin coal with a heating value of 11,800 BTUs per pound. The following emission factors are provided. The NOX emission factor is 85 lb per ton. The SO2 emission factor is 72 pounds per ton. At full load, the coal consumption rate is 55 tons per hour. What is most nearly the NOX emission rate in tons hour? Okay, so this is an extremely simple question to solve. In fact, the trick to this problem is only knowing what to avoid. So let's make this step one. The plant is burning 55 tons of coal per hour. We are given two emission factors. One for nitrogen oxides or NOx and one for sulfur dioxide or SO2. But since the question only cares about NOx, that SO2 number is just there to distract us. We also don't need to know the power output of the plant, the plant's thermal efficiency, or even the heating value of the coal. Getting rid of all these extra distractions makes the problem look a lot more simple. So, step two, calculations. Multiply the coal flow rate by the NOX emission factor. Then convert that to tons per hour. So we have 85 pounds per ton time 55 tons per hour. Finally times one ton over 2,000 lb. Making sure our units cancel leaves us with tons per hour, which is what the problem is asking for. And this calculates out to just about 2.33 tons hour, which is the closest to our third answer over here. Quick multiplication, quick unit conversion, and we're done. Again, the real test of this type of problem is not the math, but knowing what you need and what you don't. And that's it.
